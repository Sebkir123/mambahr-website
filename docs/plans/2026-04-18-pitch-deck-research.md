## Hero stat — the $1 : $6 ratio

> "For every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services."
> — **Julien Bek, Partner, Sequoia Capital.** ["Services: The New Software"](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/), March 2026.

This is the single most important sentence in the deck. It converts a vague "AI will eat services" instinct into a quantified market argument. Sequoia's own partner said it, so we're not carrying the burden of proof alone.

**Where it appears in the deck:** slides 1 (Problem) and 2 (Why Now).

**Partner pushback to prepare for:** "Is the 6x ratio uniform across HR?" — Honest answer: the essay is category-agnostic. For HR specifically the ratio is directionally correct (SHRM ratio × HR generalist wages vs. HRIS per-seat pricing) but we should be ready to concede we're extrapolating from Sequoia's cross-sector number until we commission our own benchmark. Frame it as consistent with the Sequoia thesis, not identical.

## The real TAM — total US HR labor spend

US HR professional headcount: **~720K "Human Resources Specialists"** + larger count for all HR roles (4.2M total HR-adjacent workforce, widely cited industry estimate from SHRM + BLS combined). [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/human-resources-specialists.htm).

Average HR Generalist salary (2025–2026 data):

| Source | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| [Salary.com (Dec 2025)](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/listing/hr-generalist-salary) | **$71,704** avg base |
| [ZipRecruiter (Apr 2026)](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Hr-Generalist-Salary) | **$65,839** avg base |
| [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career/human-resources-generalist/salaries) | **$67,727** avg base |

| Aggregate range | **$65K–$95K** (90th pctile $93,500) |

On the deck we use **$75K–$95K** for a single HR generalist. Fully loaded (benefits, taxes, tooling) that's $95K–$125K — which is the number that actually lands when talking to a CFO.

**Back-of-envelope US HR labor TAM:** 4.2M HR pros × $75K avg = **$315B** annually. This is the number Sequoia would call "the full insourced + outsourced labor spend in the category." It's what we're actually pricing against.

## HR-to-employee ratio (mid-market)

- **1.22 HR professionals per 100 employees** — mid-market (medium-sized orgs). [SHRM 2025 Benchmarking Reports](https://www.shrm.org/about/press-room/shrm-releases-2025-benchmarking-reports--how-does-your-organization-compare-).
- Standard range: **1:50 to 1:100** — [SHRM: How Many HR Staff Members Is Best?](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/how-many-hr-staff-members-is-best-shrm).
- Also useful: [CompanySights HR to Employee Ratio Guide 2025](https://www.companysights.com/resources/hr-to-employee-ratio-benchmarking-guide).

**Translation for the deck:** Every 100 hires forces another HR admin. A Series B at 300 people carries ~3-4 HR staff at $75-95K each = $225K–$380K/year in pure HR labor. That's the wedge.

**The argument:** Rippling is priced as SaaS at 36x. If it pivots to selling outcomes, it cannibalizes its own per-seat revenue. Its investors won't let that happen. That's our structural moat — incumbents *can't* race us here, not *won't*.

## Autonomy positioning — legal & diligence

We do **not** claim "no human in the loop." That claim is both legally thin (FMLA/ADA/Title VII decisions require a documented human decision-maker) and will be torn apart by sophisticated HR buyers. Harvey, Sierra, and Decagon all have escalation paths — we should too, and we should say so.

Defensible framing, used on slide 5:

> **Human in the approval loop, not the work loop.**

Three tiers, explicit on the deck:

1. **Auto-execute** — routine, within-policy work (policy Q&A, PTO accrual, day-1 onboarding, access provisioning). The agent owns this end-to-end.
2. **Draft → approve** — material decisions (offer letters, comp changes, promotions, leave exceptions, review cycles). The agent drafts + verifies; a human signs off before execution.
3. **Human-led** — high-stakes decisions (terminations, severance, PIPs, investigations, anything with litigation risk). The agent assists with drafting, research, and audit trail only — never decides.

This is the version that survives a CHRO's legal review *and* Sequoia's "autopilot, not copilot" framing — because the autopilot metaphor itself has a human pilot monitoring and overriding.
